# Little Harbor: design document

A top-down pixel-art fishing and exploration game for phones (primarily iPhone 14, Safari). Inspired by Stardew Valley (skills, crafting, villagers) and early Pokemon (menus, bag screen, forest route design). Everything below describes what is **already built** unless it is under *Not built yet*.

## How to play (controls)
- **D-pad** moves (hold to repeat); keyboard: arrows or WASD. Walking takes a step every 0.16 s while a direction is held; the boat sails faster (a step every 0.09 s). A swiftness potion gives the old speed (0.11 s on foot, 0.06 s by boat). They are set by `STEP_MS` and `SWIFT_MS` in `js/systems/stats.js`, and the phone D-pad and the keyboard share one held-direction timer in `js/input.js`, so they move at exactly the same speed (the PC no longer depends on the computer's own key-repeat rate). **Use** button (Space/Enter) interacts with the tile you face.
- **Turning and tapping** (`js/input.js`): pressing a direction you are not facing only **turns** you on the spot (you start walking only if you keep holding it past `TURN_MS`, 0.12 s). Pressing the direction you already face takes **one step at once**, and if you keep holding it you walk on after `REPEAT_MS` (0.17 s), so a quick tap is exactly one tile. Held directions are kept in a list: the newest one decides, and letting go of it while an older key is still held carries on in the older direction instead of stopping. The boat works the same way. Facing a tile without moving makes it easy to line up with doors, people and water.
- **Menu (☰ button top-right of the picture, or Escape):** Resume, New game, Save game, Load game. The same menu opens as the **title screen** when the game starts (Continue instead of Resume). **Save game:** *Save to a file* (downloads `little-harbor-dayN.json`), *Show a save code* (text beginning `LH1:` to copy and paste) and three **slots** kept in the browser (`little-harbor-v1-slot-1..3`). **Load game:** from a file, a pasted code or a slot. Loading checks the save is a Little Harbor save and not from a newer game (a friendly sentence if not), keeps the game it replaces as `little-harbor-v1-backup-before-load`, writes the new one and reloads the page; *New game* keeps the old one as `...-backup-new-game`. After a load or a new game the page reloads straight into the game (`sessionStorage` key `lh-started`; the tests set it to skip the title). No reminders to save. Code: `js/ui/titlemenu.js`.
- **Opening cinematic (N10):** plays when the game opens, before the title menu, once per visit (`sessionStorage` key `lh-intro`). Looking up at a blue sky with clouds and two gulls, the view tilts down to the horizon while the title "Little Harbor", built from pieces of driftwood with seaweed hanging off it, rises out of the sea, and a sailing ship seen from behind sails forward towards the horizon (about 15 seconds), at sunrise with the sun rising on the right and its light on the water. A small credit line ("Created by Jack Cathro with the use of Claude AI") sits under the title. As the ship sails into the distance the home menu (Continue, New game, Load game; Save game is only in the ☰ pause menu) appears over the scene, which keeps playing behind it; a tap or key skips straight to the menu. Drawn in code on its own canvas. Code: `js/ui/intro.js`.
- Buttons: a small row with **Deck** (sailing a bigger ship) and **Sleep** (only on the boat or at home), and under it the main row **Bag** (B), **Quests** (Q), **Skills** (K), **Map** (M) and **Menu**. Quests opens the quest log. Menu opens the character summary (your portrait, day, gold, health, stamina, boat, what you carry, best skills) with **Save game** and **Load game** buttons, which open the same save choices as the ☰ button (a file, a save code or one of three slots).
- Facing water from the boat and pressing Use casts a line; when the bite alert appears, press Use again within about a second.

## World
- Map is **320 x 240 tiles**, 16 px per tile, camera 11 x 13 tiles. Tile type numbers are listed at the end.
- Built from a hand-drawn sketch at roughly 1 tile per 7 sketch units. Sea is fully connected: every island and the mainland coast can be reached by boat.
- The original village island was generated in its own local coordinates and then shifted by (OX=124, OY=91). Old saves are migrated by `S.wv` (now 3: version 3 moved the hidden rooms below the sea, so a saved station inside the home room moves 24 rows down and saved tree/treasure records in the Darkwood move 258 rows down).

### Separate maps (zones)
These are hidden rectangles of the main grid, in **rows 246-299, below the sea you can reach** (the map is 320 x 420 tiles: `WH` = 240 rows are the real world, rows 240-419 are only the hidden rooms; boats stop at row 239 and the camera never shows the hidden rows from outside). They look like water from outside and the area beyond their edge is drawn black from inside. Entities and buildings are only drawn when the player is in the same zone (`zoneOf`).
- **Home interior**: x 300-311, y 250-259. Entered through the player house's door tile; exit by facing the interior door.
- **The Darkwood**: x 100-147, y 262-297 (it is drawn in its own coordinates y 4-39 and placed `FY` = 258 rows lower, so the numbers below are the forest's own: add 258 to every y to find them on the grid). Dark forest with a light radius around the player. South gate arrives at (123,38), north gate at (136,5). Both ends are a red torii gate three tiles wide (a post on each side, `43`/`44` in the middle; pressing Use facing any of the three goes through). Overworld gates: south at (36,149) by the woodcutters, north at (31,110), at the end of the road from the Mountain Town (you step out at (31,109)). - **Temple Tower shrine room**: x 156-166, y 250-259 (zone 10), entered from the pagoda's south door at (212,204); exit by facing the door at the bottom.

## Player and stats
- Health max 50 (+4 per Combat level). Stamina max 100. FireRed-style HP and STA bars; HP bar goes green, yellow, red.
- Stamina costs: walk 0 and sail 0 (playtest #4: moving is free; the `STAM` rows stay at 0), cast 2, swing 5, storm 0.5, gather 3, forage 1.5 (storm is unused until storms exist).
- Zero health: you wake on your boat with half health and stamina and lose 10% of your gold. Zero stamina: you move **much more slowly** (`EXH_MS` and `EXH_GLIDE` in `js/systems/stats.js`: a step every 0.30 s on foot, 0.17 s by boat; a swiftness potion brings back the normal pace, boots do not help) and cannot cast, swing, chop, mine or pick.
- Sleeping (boat or home) restores everything, advances the day, regrows resources, resets the test ghost.
- Character look: skin, hair, shirt, jacket colours, hair style (short, long, spiky, bun, ponytail, bald; `look.hstyle`, overlays in `HS` in `js/render/sprites.js`), hat (cap, straw, none), backpack and jacket toggles. 16x20 sprite module (`CharacterSprite`) with walk frames and a fishing pose. Chosen on first launch only.

## Skills (Stardew-style, levels 0-10)
XP thresholds (total XP for levels 1-10): 100, 380, 770, 1300, 2150, 3300, 4800, 6900, 10000, 15000.

| Skill | XP sources | Perk per level |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fishing | each catch (5 to 100) | 3% faster bites, better rare fish odds |
| Combat | hits (2), kills (20) | +8% damage, +4 max health |
| Sailing | 0.25 per tile sailed | 6% less travel stamina |
| Mining | ore nodes | 1 fewer swing per 3 levels, 4% extra ore |
| Woodcutting | trees | 1 fewer swing per 3 levels, 4% extra logs |

## Items, bag, equipment
- All items live in one data table (`ITEMS`): add a row to add an item. Kinds: armour, boat, claim, fish, food, forage, garden, ingredient, jewel, material, potion, prefab, rod, station, tool, weapon.
- Bag (batch F1, `js/ui/bag.js`, from Jack's sketch): eight sections down the left (`SECTIONS`: Home Items, Ingredients, Materials, Food, Weapons, Clothing, Tools, Keepsakes) and a five-wide grid of icon squares on the right; a name and description box below; an Equipment button and Back. Rings and amulets are in Clothing. Tap a square to select it, tap again (or Enter) for Eat / Drink / Place / Equip / Unequip, or to read a letter. Equipped things show an E. Keys: arrows move, Q / E or Tab change section, Esc closes. Shops use the same layout later (F2).
- Boats are not in the bag: they are switched at Murl's boatyard. Rods and boats are bought in order; boats can be bought or built in any order.
- Test sword is granted once (see testing flags).

## Fishing
Cast costs 2 stamina. Wait 1.5-4.5 s multiplied by rod wait and (1 - 0.03 x Fishing level). Bite window about 1.1 s; press Use to catch. Boat tier gives a 30%% chance per tier of a second roll keeping the better fish; Fishing level shifts rarity weights toward rarer fish. (An earlier reel-in minigame with rod power and fish pull exists in git history/backups; the data fields `power` and `pull` are still in the item table for when fishing difficulty is reworked.)

Fish: Sardine (sells 3), Mackerel (sells 6), Bream (sells 15), Tuna (sells 60), Golden koi (sells 300). (Halved in playtest #2.)

Rods: Driftwood rod (0g, power 1, wait x1), Bamboo rod (80g, power 1.3, wait x0.85), Fiberglass rod (600g, power 1.7, wait x0.7), Carbon rod (4000g, power 2.3, wait x0.55).

## Gathering
- **Trees** (axe): soft wood 2 swings, medium 3, hard 5; stumps regrow in 3 nights. Wood type is derived from tile coordinates.

- **Flax** and **herbs/mushrooms** (knife): one snip. Flax regrows in 2 nights, herbs in 3. Treasure bundles never regrow.
- Resource state is stored in `S.cut` keyed by tile index.

## Combat (early)
Only one enemy exists: the **spirit** on the graveyard isle southwest of the village pier (about 12 HP, hits for 6 every ~2 s when adjacent, chases within 7 tiles). - **The enemy table (3.1):** every real-time enemy is a row of `ENEMY_TYPES` in `js/data/enemies.js` (name, hp, damage, wait between hits, speed, chase range, island or free roaming, art, xp, gold, drops, respawn, death message) and every enemy that exists is an entry of `ENEMIES` in `js/systems/combat.js` (the spirit `G` is the first). `spawnEnemy('id',x,y,{group})` adds one; `enemyAt(x,y)`, `swing(e)`, `enemyDie(e)`, `enemiesTick()` and `enemiesMorning()` do the rest, and drawing goes through `ENEMY_ART` (`js/render/entities.js`). ## Versions and caching
Every push to `main` that passes the tests is published to GitHub Pages. The publish step stamps the commit's short id on every script and style link (`?v=abc1234`) and on `BUILD`, so phones and browsers load the new files and not old saved copies. The welcome message on loading a save ends with `Version abc1234.` (it says `Version dev.` when the game is run from a downloaded copy), so you can tell which version you are playing.

## Scenes (small cutscenes)
- `SCENES.some_id=[steps]` in `js/systems/scenes.js`; `runScene(id,onEnd)` plays one and a trigger row starts one with `{scene:'some_id'}`. Steps: wait, fade out/in, walk (the player or a named person, one tile at a time), face, tp (jump), say (a `DLG` dialogue), line (one spoken line), pan (camera to a tile, or back to the player), shake, and any trigger action such as set, give, gold, toast, quest or move. While a scene plays the player cannot move, use things, sleep or open menus (the buttons dim); a **Skip** button (top left) or Escape jumps to the end with every remaining step applied at once, so people end up where the scene would leave them and flags are still set. The camera and fade are always released at the end. `{walk}` is for the scene only (people wander back to their usual spot); `{move}` puts a person somewhere for good.

## Gates and locks
- `GATES` in `js/systems/gates.js`: a row is `{id, flag, cells:[[x,y],..], closed:tile, msg, needs, opens}`. Until its `flag` (a story switch) is set, the cells are swapped for a blocking tile (36 boulder, 12 stone wall, 54 jagged rock in the sea, 101 pirate-ship water ...), so walking and sailing are blocked by the ordinary tile rules; when the flag is set the original tiles come back for good. The state is just the flag, so it is saved and rebuilt at load, and a trigger's `{set:'flag'}` opens it live. `needs:{eq:'pickaxe'}` (equipped tool) or `{item:'id'}` lets the player open it by facing it and pressing Use; otherwise it says `msg` and waits for the story. A cell with the player, the boat, a person or an animal on it stays open until they leave. No gate is used by the game yet; quests add rows.

## Seagull letters
- `LETTERS.some_id={from,title,text}` in `js/systems/letters.js` (a blank line in `text` starts a new paragraph). A trigger row `{letter:'some_id'}` sends it: a seagull flies in, lands beside the player, the letter opens on parchment, and closing it sends the gull off and keeps the letter in the bag's **LETTERS** pocket (`S.letters`, saved) to read again by tapping it. A letter you already have is never sent twice. It runs as a scene, so the player cannot move meanwhile and Skip keeps the letter without showing it. Scene steps `{gull:'in'}`, `{gull:'out'}` and `{read:'id'}` (open the paper only) are available to other scenes. The words are placeholders until Jack's text is added from `STORY_TEXT.md`.

## World variants
- `VARIANTS` in `js/world/variants.js` is the one table of places that change with a story switch (flag). A row is `{id, flag, apply:(on)=>..}` for a place built by its own code (today: `pirate_camp` on `pirates_here` -> `applyPirates`, `sky_temple` on `sky_damaged` -> `applySky`) or `{id, flag, tiles:[[x,y,tile],..]}` for a simple swap (the first look of each cell comes back when the flag is off). Every row is applied at load and again whenever `setFlag` changes a flag (only a row whose state really changed is re-applied), so a trigger's `{set:'flag'}`, a scene or sleeping changes the world live and a saved flag keeps its variant after a reload. After a change `unstick()` moves a player, boat or person standing on a tile that now blocks them to the nearest tile they can stand on. The overnight pirate arrival (`ARRIVALS`) now only sets `pirates_here`; the table does the rest. Planned rows: festival decorations, the village under attack, the Temple Tower sealed / open, the risen island.

## People by story
- `STORY_PEOPLE` in `js/systems/storypeople.js`: rows `{npc:'Name', if:{flag/noflag/has/gold/day/quest/step}, at:[x,y], f:'d', hide:1, say:[lines], dlg:'dialogue_id'}`. For each person the first row whose `if` is true is used, and with no match they are as built. `at` is in world tile coordinates (the numbers on the map) and they wander around the new spot; `hide` makes them not drawn, not in the way and impossible to talk to; `say` replaces their chat lines; `dlg` runs a dialogue instead of the chat or shop. A story-only person is a normal `NPC` entry followed by a plain `{npc:'Name', hide:1}` row (so the specific rows come first). The table is checked at load, whenever a flag or quest changes, at each morning and step, and only a person whose row really changed is touched; nothing is saved but the flags and quest the rows read. Nobody is put on the player or on a wall (nearest free tile instead). The `{move:['Name',x,y]}` trigger action still moves someone for good (saved); rows are for changes that depend on the story.

## Turn-based battles
- `FOES` and `BATTLES` in `js/data/battles.js`; the screen and rules in `js/systems/battle.js`. `startBattle('id', onEnd)` or a trigger action `{battle:'id'}` opens a full-screen battle: up to three foes drawn as people on top, you below with health and stamina, a message box, and a menu of **Attack / Item / Run**. Tap a foe to choose who you hit. Attack uses your weapon's damage and Combat level (a little random; bare hands do 2); a poison coat poisons the foe (2 a turn for 3 turns, and its hits are halved). Item uses any food or potion (a turn is used only if it did something). Run succeeds 60% of the time unless the row has `noRun`. Foes that are left then take their turns (your jewellery Ward lowers their hits); a foe with `flee:{at,msg}` runs away at that fraction of its health (the pirate captain in the story). **Win:** gold, Combat xp and drops, then the row's `win` actions; **lose:** you wake on your boat as after any knock-out (`faint()`) and `lose` runs; `ran` and `escaped` have their own action lists. The actions are the same as a trigger's. While a battle plays the player cannot move, use things or sleep and real-time enemies stand still. Rows named `test_*` appear in the testing menu (TEST_STORY) as "Fight: ...". Skill, armour and the arena/guild fighters are not turn-based yet. `BATTLE_FAST` (tests) removes the pauses.

## Encounters
- `ENCOUNTERS` in `js/systems/encounters.js`: a row is `{id, mode:'battle'|'realtime', foes:[{foe|type, at:[x,y]}], near, together, noRun, start, win:[actions]}`. A trigger or scene starts one with `{encounter:'id'}` (or any flag named in `start`), which sets `enc_<id>_on` and brings the foes in; every foe beaten sets `enc_<id>_done`, runs the `win` actions and fires the triggers waiting for `{beaten:'id'}`. **Battle mode:** each foe stands in the world as a person (a `FOES` row); face one and press Use to fight it through the battle screen (or the whole group at once with `together`, up to three). Winning, or a foe escaping, removes that figure and the progress is saved in `S.story.enc`; losing wakes you on your boat and the foes wait. **Realtime mode:** the foes are `ENEMY_TYPES` enemies in a group that chase you; beating the whole group finishes it. After a reload the foes still standing are back at full health and the beaten ones stay beaten. Foes never spawn in a wall or on the player. Rows named `test_*` appear in the testing menu (TEST_STORY) as "Encounter: ..." and appear around the player.

## Every fight is turn-based (Jack's note P6) and the battle field (P9)
- Creatures on the map (the spirit, the Darkwood centipedes, anything spawned from `ENEMY_TYPES`) still wander and chase, but they never fight in real time any more. **Touching one starts the battle screen**: it reaches the player, the player walks into it, or the player faces it and presses Use. The battle's foe is made from its `ENEMY_TYPES` row (`hit:[min,max]`, or half of `dmg` up to `dmg`; xp, gold, drops and `art` come from the same row). A win gives the rewards, takes the creature off the map (it comes back as its row says) and counts for its `group`; running away leaves it on the map waiting a moment (`cd`, about 4 seconds) and being knocked out sends it home. The old swing, bite and poison-over-time code is gone; a poison coat now poisons the foe inside the battle. Arena bouts and guild training are still real-time and will be converted later.
- **The battle field:** the battle screen is now a painted green field (sky, far hills, a treeline, grass with flowers; `drawField` in `js/systems/battle.js`) with the foes standing on it, each with a soft shadow, a name and health tag above, a gold marker over the chosen foe (no boxes), and the player at the front on the left. Creatures are drawn on the field by their map art (`ENEMY_ART`, borrowing the drawing surface for a moment: `paintCreature`, sized by `ART_SHOT`) and animate; pirates are drawn as people.

## Weapon attacks, Block and weapon skills (Jack's note P7)
- **Attack** on the battle screen opens a list of the attacks the player knows with the weapon in hand (`js/data/attacks.js`: `ATTACKS` and `WEAPON_MOVES`). **Sword** (every blade: sword, bronze, iron, greatsword): Slash, then **Lunge at Sword skill 2** (10% more damage than Slash for 5 stamina instead of 4), Quick Strike at Sword skill 3 and Power Slash at 6. **Axe** (the new **Hand axe**, 400 gold at Garrick's forge): Chop, then Bash at Axe skill 3 and Cleave at 6. **Bare hands and the wooden sword** (`wtype:'starter'`): Strike and Bash from the start. Items without a `wtype` count as swords.
- **Every attack costs stamina** (out of 100): light (Quick Strike) 2, normal (Slash, Strike, Chop, Bash) 4, heavy (Power Slash, Cleave) 8. An attack you cannot afford is greyed out. A hit is the weapon's damage x the attack's power (light 0.6, normal 1, heavy 1.6) x the weapon skill's bonus (+8% a level) x a little luck; it no longer uses the Combat skill (Combat still gives +4 max health a level, and its damage bonus still applies in the arena and guild). **Bash** has a **15% chance to stun** (the foe loses its next turn).
- **Weapon skills:** two new skills, **Sword** and **Axe**, listed on the Skills screen. Using an attack trains the weapon's skill (light 6, normal 10, heavy 16 xp; the starter set trains none), and reaching a level that teaches an attack says so ("You learned Quick Strike!"). The numbers are first guesses to tune at the playtest.
- **Block** (new menu entry, now Attack / Block / Item / Run) takes only 35% of the damage that turn and gives 10 stamina back, so it is how stamina is recovered in a fight.
